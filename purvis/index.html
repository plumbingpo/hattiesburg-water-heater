How Purvis Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

Most early failures start with skipped maintenance, and the habits that prevent them are simple. Flush the tank every year to clear sediment, have the anode rod inspected every few years, and operate the relief valve each year so it doesn't seize. Keep the temperature near 120 degrees to reduce scald risk and slow scale buildup. For a tankless heater in a hard-water area, plan on descaling once a year so the heat exchanger stays clean.

Age matters too. Most tank water heaters last 8 to 12 years, tankless units often reach 20 with regular descaling, and heat pump models usually last 10 to 15. If your heater is past that window and showing warning signs such as discolored hot water, rumbling during heating, dampness under the tank, or hot water that runs out sooner than it used to, a planned replacement is almost always cheaper and much easier on you than an emergency one with water on the floor.

The earlier you report a hot water problem, the cheaper it usually is. A tank that's just begun to rumble can often be fixed with a flush, yet that same buildup, ignored for a few years, can create hot spots on the tank floor and shorten the life of the tank, forcing a replacement much sooner. Save our number on your phone today so help is one call away as soon as something sounds or looks wrong. A quick call saves time, money, and ruined floors.

Hot Water Headaches Purvis Homeowners Face

Water heaters in and around Purvis face a predictable set of stresses. Depending on whether a home is on municipal water or a private well, minerals can be a real factor: calcium and magnesium settle to the bottom of a tank as sediment, forcing the burner or lower element to work harder, and that's what causes the rumbling and popping you may hear during a heating cycle. In tankless units, the same minerals build scale inside the heat exchanger, which is why annual descaling is worth keeping up with where the water is hard.

Age matters just as much. Many homes around here still have a tank that was installed a decade or more ago, and beyond about ten years, the anode rod has usually been consumed and corrosion begins attacking the steel from the inside. The area has a mix of fuel types, gas tanks in some houses and electric tanks in others, so our technicians handle everything from worn thermocouples and sooty burners to tripped high-limit switches and dead elements.

Code requirements add another layer. Replacing an older heater frequently means bringing the install up to current code: an expansion tank if the water system is closed, a relief valve drain line piped to an approved spot, venting that drafts properly on gas units, plus straps and a pan where required. Well water brings its own challenges, including heavier sediment, iron staining, and the rotten-egg smell that sometimes develops when certain water chemistry reacts with a standard anode rod. Every one of these problems is fixable, and most are much cheaper to address early. If your water heater is doing anything unusual, a quick diagnostic visit can keep a small problem from turning into a flooded floor. Give us a call at (601) 230-0332 to set up a visit.

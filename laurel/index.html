Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Laurel

A little routine care makes all the difference between a water heater that lasts and one that fails early. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: an annual sediment flush, an anode inspection every few years, a yearly test of the T&P valve, a temperature setting around 120 degrees, and annual descaling for tankless units on hard water, so small issues get caught early.

How old is your water heater? The rating plate on the tank usually encodes the month and year it was built, and that date tells you a lot. Most tank models reach 8 to 12 years, heat pump units 10 to 15, and well-maintained tankless units around 20. If your heater is past that range and is also showing rusty water, rumbling, or moisture at the base, it's a strong candidate for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than a rushed emergency install after a leak.

Minor issues don't stay minor for long. Consider a spent anode rod: replacing it is a short, affordable repair, but if nobody replaces it, the water starts eating the tank itself, and suddenly a minor fix has become a full replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to keep our number in their phone and call at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves money and spares you a surprise cleanup.

What Goes Wrong With Local Water Heaters

Most water heater failures we see in Laurel start with what's in the water. Depending on whether a home is on municipal water or a private well, minerals can be a real factor: dissolved minerals drop out as sediment on the tank floor, insulating the water from the burner, which produces the rumbling and popping you may hear during a heating cycle. In tankless units, the same minerals build scale inside the heat exchanger, and that's why annual descaling matters so much where the water is hard.

Age is the other big factor. Plenty of homes in the area still have the original tank, and once a tank is past the ten-year mark, the anode rod is usually spent and corrosion begins attacking the steel from the inside. Homes here also run on a mix of fuel types, natural gas and propane units in some homes and electric in others, so our technicians handle everything from worn thermocouples and sooty burners to burned-out elements and failed thermostats.

Code requirements add another layer. Swapping out an older heater often means updating the installation along the way: a thermal expansion tank if the plumbing is a closed system, a T&P discharge line piped to an approved spot, a flue that drafts correctly on gas units, plus straps and a pan where required. Well water brings its own challenges, including extra sediment, iron staining, and the sulfur odor that occasionally develops when certain water chemistry reacts with a standard anode rod. Every one of these problems is fixable, and most cost far less to address early. If your water heater is doing anything unusual, a diagnostic visit from our team can keep a small problem from turning into a flooded floor. Call (601) 230-0332 and we'll take a look.

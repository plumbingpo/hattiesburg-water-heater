Most gas water heaters in Hattiesburg, MS work the same basic way. Gas enters through a control valve that doubles as the thermostat and feeds a burner beneath the tank, and the hot exhaust travels up a flue through the middle of the tank and out through the vent. A thermocouple, or a flame sensor on newer electronic models, tells the valve a flame is present; without that signal, the valve closes for safety. The design is dependable, but it has a few predictable weak spots. Thermocouples wear out, pilot tubes and burner ports clog, the flame arrestor screen at the base packs up with lint and dust, control valves fail, and vents lose their draft.

Diagnosing a gas water heater properly means checking combustion, not just parts. Across Hattiesburg, our technicians measure thermocouple output in millivolts, study the pilot and burner flame for color and shape, read gas pressure at the valve, and test draft at the hood so we know exhaust is leaving the house. We check the combustion chamber for soot and scorching, which point to a blocked flue or poor combustion air, and we test for carbon monoxide near the heater. Keep one rule in mind: a gas smell means leaving the house and calling the gas utility from outside before anything else, and Hattiesburg Water Heater can take over from there.

The fix is usually straightforward once the cause is known. A new thermocouple or flame sensor, a cleaned pilot tube and burner, a cleared flame arrestor screen, or a replacement gas control valve gets most heaters running again the same day. Vent problems, like a disconnected connector or a flue pipe without enough rise, are corrected on the spot. If sediment is rumbling in the tank, we flush it, and if the anode rod is spent, we can replace it. When replacement is the smarter move, we install new gas heaters sized to your household's first-hour demand, with proper atmospheric, power vent, or direct vent venting, a drip leg on the gas supply, and the expansion tank, drain pan, and strapping your local code calls for. Symptoms That Mean It's Time to Call

Catching a symptom early is often the difference between a quick part swap and a tank that gives out on the garage floor. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Leaks at the drain valve, the fittings, or the bottom of the tank
  • Exhaust odor, rust, or moisture around the draft hood and vent pipe
  • Soot or scorch marks around the burner access door
  • Showers that go cold sooner than they did a year ago
  • Loud rumbling from the bottom of the tank
  • A silent heater with no flame and no hot water
  • A rotten-egg odor near the water heater, in which case get out and call the gas company first
  • A pilot light that will not stay lit

Why Hire a Professional for Gas Water Heater Services?

Consider the risks first. Every gas water heater involves fuel piping, an open flame, and exhaust that contains carbon monoxide. A loose flare or threaded joint can leak gas, a vent that is disconnected or poorly sloped can spill exhaust back into the room, and a gas valve installed without a leak test is a risk you cannot see. Then there is the diagnostic side: a pilot that goes out can mean a weak thermocouple, a dirty pilot orifice, a clogged flame arrestor, a draft problem, or a failing gas valve, and working through them by guesswork is slow and costly.

Professional service gets you a correct answer and a safe appliance. We confirm thermocouple output, gas pressure, burner flame, and draft with real instruments, not trial and error. Every gas connection we make is tested for leaks before we leave, and vent connectors are secured with the right slope and clearances. Tips to Keep Your Water Heater Healthy

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Make sure your home has CO alarms that work, especially with gas appliances
  • Flush a few gallons from the drain valve periodically to control sediment
  • Keep the thermostat around 120 degrees to save energy and reduce scalding risk
  • Give the heater breathing room and keep flammables well away from it
  • Replace a spent anode rod before the tank itself starts to rust
  • Keep the air intake at the bottom of the heater free of lint and dust
  • Make sure the vent pipe stays connected, sloped upward, and free of rust

Keeping Your Columbia Water Heater Running Longer

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, lift the T&P valve lever once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which limits scalding risk and cuts down on scale. Tankless owners should plan on a descaling flush every year in hard-water areas.

Equipment age plays a big role. With regular maintenance, a tank water heater usually runs 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts acting up, like discolored hot water, popping noises, or a damp spot around the base, it's smart to start planning a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule is cheaper and is far less stressful than scrambling for a new heater after a leak.

Minor issues don't stay minor for long. Take a spent anode rod: swapping it out is a short, affordable repair, but if nobody replaces it, the water starts eating the tank steel, and just like that a minor fix has become a full replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to save our number in their phone and call at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and spares you a surprise cleanup.

Hot Water Headaches Columbia Homeowners Face

Whatever type of water heater your Columbia home has, it tends to fail in a predictable way. Gas tank heaters tend to have trouble with pilot assemblies and thermocouples, sooty burners, and venting that doesn't draft. Electric units lose elements and thermostats, particularly when sediment piles up around the lower element. Tankless heaters show error codes for scale buildup, ignition failures, and flow sensor problems, most of which trace back to skipped descaling. Hybrid heat pump units depend on a clean filter and a free-flowing condensate line, and they struggle in cramped closets. We service all four types regularly and stock the common parts to finish most repairs in one trip.
